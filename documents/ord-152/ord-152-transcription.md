ORDINANCE No. 152.

An Ordinance consolidating Short street and Canal and Reservoir street, and
changing the name of the streets so consolidated to Bellevue avenue.

The Mayor and Council of the City of Los Angeles, do ordain as follows:

Section 1. That the street extending from upper Main street to Canal and
Reservoir street, and heretofore designated as Short street, and the street
extending from Short street to the western boundary of the city, and heretofore
designated as Canal and Reservoir street be, and the same are hereby
consolidated and declared to be one street, and the name of the same is hereby
changed to Bellevue avenue, by which name it shall be hereafter known and
designated.

Sec. 2. The Clerk of the Council shall certify to the passage of this
ordinance, and cause the same to be published once in the Los Angeles Daily
HERALD, and thereupon and thereafter it shall take effect and be in force.

I hereby certify that the forgoing ordinance was adopted by the Council of the
City of Los Angeles, at its meeting of January 13, 1885.

W. W. ROBINSON,
Clerk of the Council of the City of Los Angeles.

Approved this 22d day of January, 1885.

E. F. SPENCE,
Mayor.

Published January 24, 1884.

## Notes on the reading

- "forgoing" for "foregoing" in the certification is the book's spelling and is
  kept.
- ⚠ **"Published January 24, 1884"** is a printer's slip for **1885**. The
  ordinance was adopted January 13, 1885 and approved January 22, 1885; a
  publication eleven months earlier is impossible. The same slip appears on the
  ordinances on either side (No. 151, "Published January 20, 1884", and No. 153,
  "Published January 25, 1884"), so the compositor was running a year behind
  through this gathering.
- The **enacting title** says "changing the name of the streetS so consolidated";
  the volume's LIST OF ORDINANCES shortens this to "Consolidating Short street
  and Canal and Reservoir street, and changing name to Bellevue avenue".
- The two extents in Section 1 are **identifications, not qualifiers**: each
  clause says which street is meant ("the street extending from … and heretofore
  designated as Short street"), and then Section 1 changes "the name of the
  same". Nothing is carved out of either street.

## What the rest of the volume says about the same ground

- **Resolution No. 313** (p. 397), *"defining the limits of Short street from
  Buena Vista street westerly to Canal and Reservoir street and dedicating the
  same to public uses as a street."* So Short street crossed Buena Vista between
  Upper Main and Canal and Reservoir.
- **Resolutions Nos. 232, 246, 309 and 310** all grade **Buena Vista street
  "between High and Short"** / "from Short street to High" — so Short and High
  were adjacent cross streets of Buena Vista. High Street became **Walters
  Street** by Ordinance No. 257 of this same volume.
- The GENERAL INDEX's Bellevue avenue entry (printed p. 636) gives grade
  resolutions at **Buena Vista street** (Res. 352, 427), **New High street**
  (471), **Victor street** (479), **from Montreal street to Sumner place** (480),
  **Toppan avenue** (483), **Lomitas street** (484), **Centennial avenue** (453)
  and **Rosas street** (352, 473) — a long westward run, which is what a street
  consolidated out of two should look like.
- **Ordinance No. 232** (p. 169) vacates **Grasshopper street**, described as
  running from "the northeast line of Bellevue avenue" 884 feet north-easterly
  "to westerly line of present Pearl street", across lots 510 and 511 of the
  **Canal and Reservoir Company's land** (Book 2, page 210, Miscellaneous
  Records). That is the Canal-and-Reservoir end of this same corridor.
