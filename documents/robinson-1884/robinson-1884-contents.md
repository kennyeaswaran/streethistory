## 0a. ⭐⭐⭐ THE NUMBERS IN THIS BOOK ARE ROBINSON'S

**Ordinance No. 104** (p. 318, adopted November 1883):

> **SECTION 1.** Hereafter, all ordinances of the City of Los Angeles, and all
> resolutions of the Mayor and Council of the City of Los Angeles, shall be
> numbered consecutively **in accordance with the numbering of the ordinances
> and resolutions, in the compilation of the ordinances and resolutions compiled
> by W. W. Robinson**, and it shall be the duty of the Clerk of the Council to so
> number said ordinances and resolutions hereafter adopted, after the same shall
> be adopted and approved, and before the same are published.

Before November 1883 the City of Los Angeles **did not number its ordinances and
resolutions at all**. Numbers 1–103 and 1–221 were assigned by the compiler and
then ratified by the Council. Consequences:

- Any citation of "Ordinance No. N" for 1878–83 is a citation of **this book**.
  A contemporary newspaper could not report the number, and
  `documents/lah-1883-09-02` indeed reports Ordinance 97's contents with none.
- The series is not chronological. **Ordinance 77 (adopted Mar. 19, 1883) comes
  before Ordinance 76 (adopted Mar. 24)**, and resolutions 190–199 and 209–213
  are printed out of numeric order (see §2).
- **Teed 1887, Vol. III begins at No. 114** — it continues this series. The
  "New Series" of `documents/ord-1613` and `documents/ord-4093` is a later
  restart.

## 0b. HOW THIS TRANSCRIPTION WAS MADE, AND HOW FAR IT IS VERIFIED

The PDF has no text layer. Every page was rendered at 200 dpi (the scan's own
resolution) and OCR'd with tesseract; then:

- **§1 and §2 below — the volume's own lists of ordinances and resolutions —
  were read line by line off the page images by eye.** They are transcriptions,
  not OCR.
- **They were independently cross-checked** against a second list built from the
  333 ordinance and resolution HEADINGS in the body (pp. 3–362), each with the
  printed folio it stands on. **The two agree on every number and every page.**
  Where the OCR of a heading disagreed with the sequence, the page image was
  read by eye; all such cases are recorded in §3.
- **§4, the subject index proper, is NOT fully transcribed.** What is given
  there is the street roster and the renaming cross-references, read by eye,
  plus a statement of what is left. Everything in the index about a street's
  grade or improvement is already in §2 in better order.

### 4d. What is left undone in the index

Everything that is not a street: licensing, water and zanja rates, fire limits,
assessment and taxation, elections, salaries, sewers by name, the Council's own
procedure. About two thirds of the index's roughly 1,600 lines. It is
transcribable the same way — the page images are rendered and the OCR is on disk
in the working directory — but nothing in it bears on street names, and §§1–2
above supersede it for finding any instrument in the book.
