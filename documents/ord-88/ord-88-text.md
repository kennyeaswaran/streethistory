ORDINANCE No. 88.

AN ORDINANCE changing the name of Polyxena street to Clay street.

The Mayor and Council of the City of Los Angeles do ordain as follows:

SECTION 1. That the name of that street in the City of Los Angeles, known and
designated as Polyxena street, be and the same is hereby changed to Clay street.

SEC. 2. All ordinances and parts of ordinances in conflict with this ordinance
are hereby repealed.

I hereby certify that the foregoing ordinance was adopted by the Council of the
City of Los Angeles, at its meeting of June 25th, A. D. 1883.

                                        W. W. ROBINSON,
                    Clerk of the Council of the City of Los Angeles.

Approved this 26th day of June, A. D. 1883.

                                        C. E. THOM,
                                                    Mayor.

Published June 30th, 1883.

## Reading notes (not part of the text)

- **There is no publication section.** Almost every other ordinance in the
  volume has a section directing the Clerk to certify and publish; this one goes
  straight from Sec. 2 to the certification. Transcribed as printed.
- **No extent** — the bare shape the amendment calls unqualified.
- **But the volume places the street anyway.** Resolution No. 150 (p. 253), two
  pages earlier, is "of intention to improve and grade Polyxena street", and its
  assessment district (p. 254) runs *"Commencing at the point of intersection of
  the center line of Polyxena street with the north boundary of Fourth street,
  and running thence … northerly 610.50 feet to the south boundary of Third
  street"*. **Polyxena Street ran north–south between Third and Fourth streets,
  610.5 feet long**, and it had a curb only on its east side, a gutter on its
  west and *"no sidewalks"*.
