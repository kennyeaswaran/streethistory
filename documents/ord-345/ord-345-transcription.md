ORDINANCE NO. 345.

An Ordinance changing the name of Virgin street.

The Mayor and Council of the City of Los Angeles do ordain as follows:

Section 1. That the name of that certain street in the City of Los Angeles
known as Virgin street, shall be and is hereby declared to be changed to Alpine
street.

Sec. 2. The Clerk of the Council shall certify to the passage of this ordinance
and shall cause the same to be published once in the Los Angeles Daily Tribune,
and thereupon and thereafter it shall take effect and be in force.

I hereby certify that the foregoing ordinance was adopted by the Council of the
City of Los Angeles at its meeting of August 22, 1887.

FREEMAN G. TEED,
Clerk of the Council of the City of Los Angeles.

Approved this 30th day of August, 1887.

L. N. BREED,
Mayor pro tem.

Published September 2, 1887.

## And what it settles about minutes copies generally

`handbook/RENAMING-SOURCES.md` §2c warns that a minutes copy of an ordinance can
be an **abridgement**, because the Sept. 1896 ledger copy of Ord. 3847 dropped
the extents the Herald printed. This is the first case in the corpus where the
two copies can be compared directly, and **they agree word for word.** The
ledger's Section 1 and Section 2 are the printed Section 1 and Section 2, with
one trivial difference: the ledger reads "known as Virgin" and the print reads
"known as Virgin street".

So §2c's warning stands as a warning — a minutes copy *can* abridge — but it is
not what happened here. The Aug. 22, 1887 ordinance is genuinely unqualified: it
states no extent in either copy, and the extent the project uses for Virgin
Street (Yale Street to Beaudry Avenue) comes from a separate grading resolution
read in the same minute session, not from the ordinance.
