# Los Angeles Herald, Oct. 11, 1888, p. 3 — the special committee's street-name list

**STREET NAMES.**
**Good Work by a Committee of the Council.**
**ORDER BROUGHT FROM CHAOS.**
**Thoroughfares That Have Had Several Names, Given One Throughout.**

For the past six or eight months a special committee of the City Council has
been at work endeavoring to arrange the names of the streets in this city, so
that there shall be no two streets of the same name, and that each street shall
have a continuous name throughout. This committee is now ready to report, and
the ordinance changing the names will probably be passed at the meeting of the
Council on Monday. Following is a summary of the changes to be adopted:

Transcribed by Claude, 2026-09-17, from the clips at full size.

**The article runs down column 1, continues at the top of column 2, and ends a
fifth of the way down column 3.** The transcription follows that order. Line
breaks between entries are the paper's; punctuation, abbreviation and spelling
are as printed, including the obvious errors flagged inline below.

**The list has two halves and they are not alike.** The first (Aztec → Bryant
below, ending at "Myrtle Ave., S. of E. Pico") is *not* alphabetical, and its
entries are mostly **consolidations** — two, three or five names on one line of
pavement folded into one. The second half is alphabetical by the *old* name and
its entries are mostly **duplicate culls** — one name borne by several unrelated
streets, all but one of which is renamed. That is exactly the two-part purpose
the lede states: "no two streets of the same name", and "each street shall have
a continuous name throughout".

**COUNT: 199 changes.** The header comment on this document previously said
"roughly three hundred"; the transcription says otherwise and the .js has been
corrected.

**⚠ THIS IS A COMMITTEE PROPOSAL, NOT AN INSTRUMENT.** It is the draft behind
Ordinance No. 48 (`documents/lah-1889-05-10`), published six months before it,
and the committee's own closing sentence invites further amendment. At least
four of the lines below are known not to have survived into the ordinance or
onto the modern map; they are flagged ⚠ where they occur.

## Abbreviations the paper uses without expanding

| As printed | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| `H. S.` / `H. survey` | the **Hancock survey** — the numbered-block cadastral survey of the city lands. The committee locates fourteen streets by Hancock block number and by nothing else. |
| `E. L. A.` | East Los Angeles — the district north-east of the river, i.e. modern Lincoln Heights, **not** the modern unincorporated East L.A. |
| `add.` / `Addition` | an addition, i.e. a recorded subdivision |
