ORDINANCE No. 207.

An Ordinance changing the names of certain streets.

The Mayor and Council of the City of Los Angeles, do ordain as follows:

Section 1. That the name as that certain street known as Elm street, and
extending from Pico street to California street, be and the same is hereby
changed to Olive street.

Sec. 2. That the name of that certain street known as William street, situated
in the Child's tract, be and the same is hereby changed to Santee street.

Sec. 3. That the name of that certain street known as William street, and
situated in the Los Angeles Improvement Company's subdivision of lots, be and
the same is hereby changed to Goss street.

Sec. 4. The Clerk of the Council shall certify to the passage of this
ordinance, and cause the same to be published once in the Los Angeles Daily
HERALD, and thereupon and thereafter it shall take effect and be in force.

I hereby certify that the foregoing ordinance was adopted by the Council of the
City of Los Angeles, at its meeting of January 6, 1886.

W. W. ROBINSON,
Clerk of the Council of the City of Los Angeles.

Approved this 14th day of January, 1886.

E. F. SPENCE,
Mayor.

Published January 15, 1886.

## Notes on the reading

- "the name **as** that certain street" in Section 1 is the book's own error for
  "the name **of**"; Sections 2 and 3 both read "of". Kept as printed.
- The Herald's copy of the same ordinance, transcribed at
  `documents/lah-1886-01-15/`, reads "Childs Tract" where the printed volume
  reads "Child's tract", and "the Los Angeles Improvement Company's subdivision
  of lots" identically. The two texts agree in substance throughout; the
  ordinance number, which the Herald item does not give, is **207**.
- **California street** is the street that became **Fourteenth Street**; the
  corpus's `california-street-14th` cites this ordinance.
- There are **three** streets in this ordinance and only **two** rows in the
  corpus. Section 3's Goss Street has no name entity — see `TASK.md`.
