# Caswell 1878 — the volume's finding aids, transcribed

*Wm. M. Caswell, comp., **Revised Charter and Compiled Ordinances and
Resolutions of the City of Los Angeles** (Los Angeles: Evening Express Steam
Printing Establishment, 1878).* Gale, *The Making of Modern Law: Primary
Sources*, doc id `DT0106137878`.

This file transcribes **both** of the volume's finding aids — the *Index to
Charter* (printed pp. 523–524) and the *Index to Ordinances and Resolutions*
(printed pp. 525–549) — plus the full text of the renaming instruments the
index points to. The volume has **no table of contents**: these two indexes are
the only finding aids it carries.

**Transcribed from 300–400 dpi renders of the Gale scan, OCR'd with tesseract
and then checked line by line against the page image.** Odd spellings
(*Castellar*, *Sansevaine*, *Polaski*, *Elisalde*, *Brundige*) are as printed.
Where the volume itself is inconsistent (it spells the same street two ways on
two pages) that is recorded, not silently fixed.

## 1. PDF page ↔ printed folio

⚠ **The offset changes twice.** Gale inserts a citation leaf at each part
boundary, and each one shifts everything after it by one.

| PDF pages | printed pages | offset | what is there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–200 | 1–200 | **0** | title, certificate, Charter, Rules, Ordinances §§1–359 |
| **201** | — | — | **Gale citation leaf** (no printed folio) |
| 202–401 | 201–400 | **+1** | Ordinances §§360–c.604 |
| **402** | — | — | **Gale citation leaf** |
| 403–551 | 401–549 | **+2** | Ordinances §§c.605–735, then both indexes |
| **552** | — | — | **Gale citation leaf** |

**So:** `printed = PDF` up to PDF 200 · `printed = PDF − 1` for PDF 202–401 ·
`printed = PDF − 2` for PDF 403–551.

### ⚠ The numbering is the compiler's, and it is SECTION numbering

This volume predates ordinance numbering in Los Angeles. Nothing in it is
called "Ordinance No. —". Caswell runs a single arabic sequence, **§1 to §736**,
printed in the outer margin beside the operative paragraph, across ordinances
*and* resolutions alike, in rough chronological order. Both indexes are keyed to
it: every entry gives **SECTION** then **PAGE**. Cite these as *Caswell 1878,
§N (p. N)* — not as ordinance numbers.

Half-sections exist: the index carries **§574½, p. 346**.

### ⚠ A missing Appendix

The compiler's note on printed p. 145 refers the reader twice to "the Appendix"
for the land ordinances of 13 Aug. 1852 and 19 May 1854. **There is no Appendix
in this copy.** The ordinances end with §736 on printed p. 522 and the Index to
Charter begins on p. 523. Either the Appendix was dropped in press or Gale's
scan omits it; the running folios are continuous, so nothing is missing from
*between* those pages.

⚠ **"Bridge street, (now Turner street) — §279, p. 153" is a puzzle.** The
index gloss is unambiguous, but §279 on p. 153 declares public *"a strip of
land fifty (50) feet wide and running in and being the prolongation of
Sansevaine street in said city, N. 84¾ deg. E. (magnetic course) from the
western line of said 'Aliso tract,' to the Los Angeles river"* — and the same
index also lists that section under **"Sansevain street prolonged, 279 · 153."**
The section's text names no Bridge street and no Turner street. Either the
compiler is telling the reader that this river-ward prolongation was locally
called Bridge street and by 1878 Turner street, or the index reference is
misplaced. **Flagged for decision; do not build a row on it.** See FINDINGS.md.

entry on p. 530, but the ordinance text at §394, p. 215 spells it **"Castelar"**
(one L). Both are the volume's own.

## ⭐⭐⭐ §394, printed p. 215 (PDF 216) — THE FEBRUARY 26, 1874 ORDINANCE

*This is the instrument the project holds only as a minutes copy in
`documents/ord-1874-02-26`. Caswell prints it, and it settles the
one-or-three question: it is **ONE ordinance making THREE renamings**.*

> **CHANGING NAMES OF STREETS.**
>
> An Ordinance changing the names of certain streets in the city of Los
> Angeles.
>
> The Mayor and Common Council of the city of Los Angeles do ordain as follows:
>
> **394** SECTION 1. That the names of the three streets in said city known
> and designated respectively as Grasshopper street, Bull street and Wasp
> street be changed as follows:
>
> Grasshopper to be named Pearl street, Bull street to be named Castelar
> street, and Wasp street to be named Yale street, and that henceforth the said
> streets be respectively known and designated by the said names of Pearl
> street, Castelar street and Yale street.
>
> SEC. 2. This ordinance shall take effect and be in force from and after its
> passage, approval and publication.
>
> Passed, session Feb. 26th, 1874.
>
> Approved.

*[Marginal side-note beside §394: **"Grasshopper, Bull and Wasp changed to
Pearl, Castelar and Yale."**]*

**What to note about this text:**

- **One instrument, three streets.** The doubt recorded against
  `documents/ord-1874-02-26` is answered: §394 is a single ordinance naming all
  three in one operative sentence.
- **It is unqualified.** No cross streets, no extent, no "from … to". The
  change is to the whole of each street as it then stood. (⚠ Per
  `handbook/RENAMING-SOURCES.md` §2c, "unqualified" is the dangerous reading —
  but here it is what a *printed compilation* says, not a clerk's abridgement
  of a ledger, which is a better witness than the minutes copy.)
- **"Castelar", one L**, in the ordinance text. The index spells it
  **"Castellar"**, twice (pp. 529, 530). The volume disagrees with itself.
- **No approval date and no publication date.** The line reads simply
  "Approved." — where the section immediately before it, §393, carries
  "Approved Feb. 27th, 1874" and a publication date. So Caswell's copy does
  **not** confirm the approval date the minutes give.
- **§393, the section immediately above it, is from the same session** — the
  Bunker Hill Avenue ordinance, "Passed, session Feb. 26th, 1874. Approved
  Feb. 27th, 1874." So the compilation preserves the sitting's order.

### §393, printed p. 215, for context — the same meeting

> **BUNKER HILL AVENUE A PUBLIC STREET.**
>
> AN ORDINANCE defining the lines of Bunker Hill avenue and declaring it a
> public street of the city of Los Angeles.
>
> The Mayor and Common Council of the city of Los Angeles do ordain as follows:
>
> **393** SECTION 1. That that certain strip or parcel of land situated in said
> city, of the uniform width of forty (40) feet, the center line of which is
> described as follows, to-wit: Commencing at a point in the northerly line of
> Fourth street, equi-distant from the points of intersection of said northerly
> line with the east line of Hope street and the west line of Charity street in
> said city; thence running at right angles to said northerly line of Fourth
> street in a northerly direction to the west line of Temple street, a distance
> of three thousand one hundred and twenty feet, be and the same is hereby laid
> out and established as a public street of the city of Los Angeles, and open
> to the public as such.
>
> SEC. 2. That said street shall be known as and designated by the name of
> Bunker Hill avenue.
>
> SEC. 3. This ordinance shall take effect and be in force from and after its
> passage, approval and publication.
>
> Passed, session Feb. 26th, 1874.
>
> Approved Feb. 27th, 1874.
>
> Published March 4th, 1874.

⭐ **Bunker Hill Avenue is minted here**, 40 ft wide, running 3,120 ft north
from Fourth Street (midway between Hope and Charity) to the west line of Temple
Street. That is a naming *and* an extent, from the same sitting as the Wasp →
Yale change, and it dates Charity street as already in place in Feb. 1874.

# 6. Three more instruments, found by grepping the OCR dump after the index was done

The index hides these: it files each of them under a head-word that does not
mention the naming. They were found with `grep -niE "shall be called|to be
called|be known and called|hereafter be known"` over `caswell-1878.txt` and
then read off the page image.

## ⭐⭐ §680, printed pp. 454–455 (PDF 456–457) — **VINE STREET → OLVERA STREET**

*Indexed only as "Olvera street, defining lines … 680 · 454".*

> **A RESOLUTION**
>
> Declaring Olvera street a public street.
>
> Be it resolved by the Council of the city of Los Angeles, as follows:
>
> *[side-note: Declared open.]* **680** SECTION 1. That the following described
> tract of land in the city of Los Angeles is hereby declared open and to be
> used as a public street of said city, and to be known as Olvera street:
>
> *[side-note: Description.]* Beginning at a point in the south side of Macy
> street, 6½ feet distant in a westerly direction from the southwest corner of
> Alameda and Macy streets, said beginning point being the northeast corner of
> the lot whereon stands school house number two, and the northwest corner of
> the lot known as the Potter lot; thence south 37° west, 184 feet along the
> division line between the school lot and the Potter lot to a point in the
> southwest corner of the Potter lot and situate on the west side of Vine and
> Olvera street; thence north 37° east, 183½ feet parallel with the east line
> of the school lot to a point in the south side of Macy street; thence south
> 73¼° east, 60 feet along the south side of Macy street, to the point of
> beginning.
>
> **The above described parcel of land, and in addition thereto that
> thoroughfare lying to the south thereof as far as the north line of the
> Plaza, and heretofore known as Vine or Olvera street, will hereafter be known
> as Olvera street.**
>
> *[Sec. 2, publication, Los Angeles Daily Star in English and Spanish;]*
> adopted at the session of **May 3d, 1877**.

⭐⭐ **A name settled, not merely a street declared.** The thoroughfare from
Macy street south to the **north line of the Plaza** had gone by *two* names —
*"Vine or Olvera street"* — and this resolution picks one. It is the same shape
as the 1897 mass settlement: not a change from A to B, but the extinguishing of
an alternative.

It also gives **Olvera street a full extent**: from the south side of Macy
street (6½ ft west of the SW corner of Alameda and Macy, at the NE corner of
the lot of **school house number two**), 184 ft on S 37° W along the division
line with the **Potter lot**, 60 ft wide, and then on south to the Plaza.

⚠ **The index files §680 TWICE, and one of them is wrong.** Index p. 542 has
*"Olive street, defining lines … 680 · 454"*, and index p. 543 has *"Olvera
street, defining lines … 680 · 454"*. §680 is the **Olvera** resolution.
Whatever section defines Olive street's lines, it is not this one.
