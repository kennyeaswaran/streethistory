ORDINANCE NO. 381.

An Ordinance changing the name of a portion of New Cincinnati street.

The Mayor and Council of the City of Los Angeles do ordain as follows:

Section 1. That the name of New Cincinnati street in the City of Los Angeles
between Temple street and Bellevue avenue be, and the same is, hereby changed
to Edgeware road.

Sec. 2. The Clerk of the Council shall certify to the passage of this ordinance,
and shall cause the same to be published once in the Los Angeles Evening
Express, and thereupon and thereafter it shall take effect and be in force.

I hereby certify that the foregoing ordinance was adopted by the Council of the
City of Los Angeles at its meeting of March 12, A. D. 1888.

FREEMAN G. TEED,
Clerk of the Council of the City of Los Angeles.

Approved this 19th day of March, A. D. 1888.

W. H. WORKMAN,
Mayor.

Published March 21, 1888.

## Notes on the reading

- **The title says "a portion of" and the text says which portion.** This is one
  of very few *qualified* renamings in the corpus whose extent is stated in
  plain cross streets rather than metes and bounds, and both cross streets are
  still on the ground under the same names: Temple Street meets modern Edgeware
  Road, and Bellevue Avenue meets modern Edgeware Road, each at 0 m in the
  project's OSM extract.
- **The publication paper is the Evening Express**, not the Daily Tribune of
  Ords. 320 and 345, and not the Herald of Ord. 1613. Three papers carry
  renaming ordinances in this corpus now. `handbook/RENAMING-SOURCES.md` §2b's
  caveat — "the ordinance names the paper it must appear in, and it is not
  always the Herald" — is confirmed a third time, and the Evening Express is the
  same house that printed this volume.
- The next ordinance on the same page, No. 382, is about the price of irrigating
  water; there is no further street matter on the leaf.

## The relationship to Ordinance No. 48 (1889)

`documents/lah-1889-05-10` — Ordinance No. 48 (New Series), adopted Apr. 22,
1889 — contains, as clause c041:

> "That the name of the street heretofore known as New Cincinnati street,
> extending **from or near Court street to Bellevue avenue** and, lying in lot 1,
> block 39, Hancock's survey, be changed to Edgeware road."

So the city changed the same name twice, fourteen months apart, over **two
different stretches**:

| | instrument | date | stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| first | **Ord. 381** | Mar. 12, 1888 | **Temple street → Bellevue avenue** |
| second | Ord. 48 (N.S.), c041 | Apr. 22, 1889 | **Court street (or near) → Bellevue avenue** |

Court Street lies **south of** Temple Street, so the 1889 clause is not a
repetition: it is the same change carried further south, sweeping in the block
the 1888 ordinance left behind. Either the earlier ordinance was thought not to
reach far enough, or the 1889 omnibus simply re-legislated the whole corridor
without checking what was already done — the corpus already has one such
re-enactment flagged at Ord. 48's clause c058 (Elm → Olive, already done by
Ord. 207 in 1886).
