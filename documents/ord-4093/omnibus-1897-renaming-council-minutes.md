## Relevant proceedings, February 1-23, 1897

This transcript follows the ordinance that became **Ordinance No. 4093 (New Series)** from its presentation to the Council on February 1 through its amended adoption on February 23.

### Transcription conventions

- Spelling, capitalization, punctuation, and grammar are retained as closely as the handwriting permits.
- Paragraphing and line endings are regularized. Ditto marks in lists are expanded.
- Square brackets contain editorial descriptions, not language in the minutes.
- The recurring manuscript abbreviation **“Mon”** is retained as written. It appears to be the clerk’s form of an honorific before a councilmember’s surname.
- PDF page numbers count from the first image in each City Clerk PDF. “Ledger page” means the number written or stamped on the manuscript leaf.

## Monday, February 1, 1897: report and draft ordinance presented

**Citation:** City of Los Angeles, Office of the City Clerk, City Council minutes, Record Series R05.557, *M02011897_01.pdf*, PDF pp. 25-27, manuscript ledger pp. 360-362. [City Clerk PDF](https://clerkapps.lacity.org/oldcfidocs/showpdf.aspx?doctype=minutes&filedir=R05.557&pdfname=M02011897_01.pdf)

### [PDF p. 25; ledger p. 360]

The following message was received from the Mayor:

I herewith submit to your Honorable Body, the report, etc., of the commission appointed by the Mayor in December, 1896, to revise the street names.

Said report is as follows:

The Commission appointed in December, 1896, by the Mayor, to revise (as far as possible) and systematize the street names of this City, beg to report as follows:

### [PDF p. 26; ledger p. 361]

The serious and discreditable confusion which existed in the names of streets had become intolerable. There were nearly three hundred duplicate street names in the City—for instance, three A streets, two First streets, and so on. A great many continuous streets had different names in different parts—some changing at almost every block.

Study of the maps of this City reveals a confusion almost incredible in a civilized community. This muddle arises from the fact that there has never been heretofore any systematic consideration of the City as a whole.

Street-naming has been done by patch-work. The owners of new subdivisions have applied whatever names they chose, apparently careless whether those names were already in use in other parts of the City.

One of the acts of this Commission has been to have framed and introduced in the Legislature in Sacramento a bill providing that hereafter no subdivision shall be recorded until the names of its streets and their connections with existent streets shall have been approved by the proper municipal authorities.

This Commission has recognized that every change of street name creates confusion and has made no changes wantonly. But such changes as have to be made can be made more cheaply now than ever in the future. After being in session six weeks, the Commission has succeeded in eliminating all duplicate names and otherwise removing the most palpable absurdities from the City map. It has also given a single name to such streets as before had from two to five names.

### [PDF p. 27; ledger p. 362]

In applying new names to streets which had to be rechristened the Commission has aimed to perpetuate historic personal names that were prominent in the first California days, or since the American occupation. It has seemed fitting that discoverers, founders, missionaries, American pioneers and old settlers of fame in the history of the State should be thus commemorated.

As a vast majority of the City streets bear English names (borrowed from the birth-place or from the family names of present residents), and as the old names had been almost entirely obliterated, the Commission has made use of many Spanish titles—taking care that they shall be appropriate, musical, not too long, and not easily mispronounced.

We herewith present the latest corrected map of Los Angeles, upon which the suggested changes are indicated in red ink. Also an ordinance, alphabetically arranged, making those changes; and We respectfully recommend the passage of said ordinance.

Action upon said message and report was deferred until Tuesday February 2d 1897, at two o’clock p.m.

Mon Toll moves that when Council adjourns it do so to meet on Tuesday February 2nd 1897, at 2 o’clock p.m.

Which motion was adopted.

## Monday, February 8, 1897: returned unsigned and reconsidered

**Citation:** City of Los Angeles, Office of the City Clerk, City Council minutes, Record Series R05.557, *M02081897_01.pdf*, PDF pp. 8-9, manuscript ledger pp. 403-404. [City Clerk PDF](https://clerkapps.lacity.org/oldcfidocs/showpdf.aspx?doctype=minutes&filedir=R05.557&pdfname=M02081897_01.pdf)

### [PDF p. 8; ledger p. 403]

The following message was received from the Mayor:

I herewith return unsigned by me the Ordinance adopted by the Council February 2nd, changing the names of certain streets in the City of Los Angeles.

The principal reason for my not signing the ordinance is the strong protest that has been made by many citizens against the ordinance as it now stands. These protestants declare that a great wrong would be worked on them if the ordinance were permitted to become a law, for the reason that they have not been permitted to appear before your Honorable Body and state their objection to certain sections of the ordinance.

Protests come from many parts of the City, particularly from the districts north of First street.

I appreciate the fact that the Committee having in charge the revising of the names of the streets of the City worked long and faithfully and received no compensation for its labors. Property owners believed that when the report of the Committee reached the Council they would have ample time in which to appear and enter their protest

### [PDF p. 9; ledger p. 404]

against any particular part.

In my opinion, the Ordinance in the main reflects credit on the Committee having its preparation in hand. It is the first substantial step toward a system of street naming in our City. I think some of the names selected for streets by the Committee would prove very unsatisfactory to old as well as new residents of the City.

I respectfully call the attention of your Honorable Body to such names as the following:— Alcantara, Cerro Gordo, Valverde, Cimarron, Chicote, Palmida, Negarte, Santiago, Quintero, Zaragoza, Juanita, Arapahoe, Escalon, Montecito, Alarcon, Omaha, Manuela, Micheltorena. These names and many others in the ordinance are easy of pronunciation to old-time residents of the City, but they would prove very troublesome to new-comers from the east unacquainted with the Spanish language.

It is for all of the foregoing reasons that I return the ordinance unsigned. I trust that your Honorable Body will see fit to make any reasonable changes in the ordinance that citizens may ask for.

Mon Toll moves that the vote upon which said ordinance changing street names was adopted be reconsidered.

Which motion was adopted by the following vote; Ayes. Messrs. Ashman, Baker, Blanchard, Grider, Hutchison, Mathuss, Toll and Pres. Silver. (8) Noes. none.

Mon Grider moves that the matter of said ordinance changing street names be made a special order for Monday February 15th, 1897, at 2 o’clock p.m. Which motion was adopted.

**Editorial note:** Two names in the mayor’s list are unusually formed in the manuscript. The clerk appears to have written **“Palmida”** and **“Negarte”** here; the February 23 committee report spells the latter **“Negrete.”**

## Monday, February 15, 1897: hearing postponed and joint committee appointed

**Citation:** City of Los Angeles, Office of the City Clerk, City Council minutes, Record Series R05.557, *M02151897_01.pdf*, PDF pp. 2-3, manuscript ledger pp. 449-450. [City Clerk PDF](https://clerkapps.lacity.org/oldcfidocs/showpdf.aspx?doctype=minutes&filedir=R05.557&pdfname=M02151897_01.pdf)

Mon Baker moves that the matter of the hearing of the report of the Commissioners for the changing of street names and of the ordinance accompanying said report, set for

### [PDF p. 3; ledger p. 450]

hearing on this day at two o’clock p.m., as a special order, be postponed until, and made a special order for Tuesday February 23rd 1897, at two o’clock p.m.

## Tuesday, February 23, 1897: committee amendments and final adoption

**Citation:** City of Los Angeles, Office of the City Clerk, City Council minutes, Record Series R05.557, *M02231897_01.pdf*, PDF pp. 31-37, manuscript ledger pp. 522-528. [City Clerk PDF](https://clerkapps.lacity.org/oldcfidocs/showpdf.aspx?doctype=minutes&filedir=R05.557&pdfname=M02231897_01.pdf)

### [PDF p. 31; ledger p. 522]

Recess until 2 o’clock P.M.

### Afternoon Session.

The Council met at 2 o’clock P.M. pursuant to adjournment. The roll was called showing the following members to be present: Messrs. Ashman, Baker, Blanchard, Grider, Hutchison, Mathuss, Nickell, Toll and Pres. Silver (9)

The matter of the report of the Commissioners

### [PDF p. 32; ledger p. 523]

for the change of street names, coming on to be heard at this hour as a special order, the same was taken up. The Special Committee of the Council appointed to meet jointly with said Commission reported as follows:

Your Special Committee appointed to confer with the Commission having in charge the changing of street names throughout the City, for the purpose of jointly entertaining the various protests and objections to the Ordinance presented by said Commission, beg leave to report as follows;

It is a matter of felicitation with your Committee that the gentlemen composing the Honorable Commission received them in such a spirit of willingness to do all in their power to adjust the differences which existed. The patient forbearing disposition evinced by the members of the Commission in the presence of misapplied personalities and irritating criticism based upon misapprehension of the Commission’s motives calls for the highest commendation on the part of your Committee. From this brief experience your Committee can form only an imperfect idea of the trying ordeal through which the Commission passed in its various deliberations extending over a period of six weeks, and it is our pleasure to here attest to the painstaking manner in which they performed the work in hand and to bespeak an expression of thanks from your Honorable Body and the citizens generally for the self sacrificing devotion of these worthy gentlemen to a gratuitous public service.

The very nature of the Commission’s work invites opposition and it is not surprising that objections were raised to some features of the final Ordinance.

### [PDF p. 33; ledger p. 524]

your Committee was impressed with the great merit of the reformation in street nomenclature as well as the careful consideration evinced in the changes made, from the fact that over three hundred and fifty changes appear while barely a dozen protests have been filed.

Upon investigation your Committee found;

1st; The Commission did not make changes wantonly except in three or four cases.

2nd; The new names suggested are to remove duplicates or to constitute the same name throughout the entire length of a street.

3rd; Of the new names chosen more than two-thirds are English and less than one-third Spanish.

4th; The City would thus have about 1250 English street names and 150 Spanish.

Having reference to the Mayor’s message herein, we find two objections urged against the Ordinance, 1st, no opportunity given protestants to be heard, and 2nd, difficulty in pronouncing some names chosen. The first objection has found its satisfaction in the joint hearing of the Commission and Committee, and will doubtless be still further gratified in any additional hearing you may grant before your Honorable Body at time of the presentation of this report and accompanying ordinance.

There is, however, much difficulty in fully meeting the second objection, not from any arbitrary desire of this joint committee to retain names to tax the new comers’ ability in either pronunciation or orthography, but rather from the unfortunate circumstances that those individuals and events whose association with the Kindergarten period of this region’s history makes it desirable and;

### [PDF p. 34; ledger p. 525]

proper that their memory should be perpetuated and revered by all, were christened with names somewhat out of date at this period. To eliminate these names is to deny all gratitude to those illustrious heroes and incidents of the past and to show a wanton lack of appreciation of their valued contribution to our present peaceful enjoyment of the “fruits of their labors.”

In the judgment of your Committee it were better a stranger in our midst encounter an impediment in pronunciation than that an entire community commit the gross indignity of ignoring the hardy heroes and worthy incidents of pioneer days. A very brief period of association with the new names will render them as familiar as those we now utter without a struggle and which seemed at first equally beyond mastery.

In this spirit your Committee beg to return herewith the original ordinance prepared by the Honorable Commission with the following changes, to-wit:

Two flagged conflicts settle:

- **Escalon/Zaragoza.** The manuscript gives Escalon→**Rossini** and
  Zaragoza→**Dana**, i.e. the Feb. 21 pairing. The Feb. 19 article's
  swapped version was OCR garble. (The signed ordinance would still be the
  final word, but the two independent Feb. 23 readings now agree.)
- **"Lacy vs. Canada → Avenue 33" is not an OCR conflict.** Both appear in
  the manuscript: Canada Street → Avenue 33 in the committee's substitution
  list, Lacy Street → Avenue 33 among the additional urgent-request changes.
  A genuine duplicate assignment inside the amendments — worth knowing when
  Lincoln Heights/Highland Park coverage arrives, since one of them must
  have been fixed later.

Also confirmed: Hawkins→Manitou **Street**, then corrected on the floor to
Manitou **Avenue** on Feb. 23 — matching the Herald Feb. 24 report of a
"clerical error."

**Change-count discrepancy, unresolved:** the committee's own report says
"over three hundred and fifty changes"; the Herald of Mar. 5 says the Burks
map will show "the 326 changes." Possibly gross vs. net after amendment.
Only the ordinance text settles it — don't cite either number as definitive.

## Items in coverage

Most were already applied from the Herald; what changes is that they can now
carry a manuscript citation, and that **every 1897-renaming entry in the data
can name the instrument: Ordinance No. 4093 (New Series), adopted Feb. 23,
1897, signed Feb. 26.**

- ✔ **Figueroa / Boylston** — Toll's floor motion changing "De La Guerra
  Street" to "Boylston Street" in the draft. Note what this implies: the
  Feb. 2 version was returned unsigned and its adopting vote reconsidered, so
  **De La Guerra was never law** — it existed only in the vetoed version and
  the draft. Boylston's entry currently dates a "De La Guerra Street" phase
  Feb.–Feb. 1897; that is now checkable against the record.
- ✔ **Georgia Street** — Silver's floor motion, "Nevada Street" → "Georgia
  Street," Feb. 23.
- ✔ **Central Avenue** — "Vine Street from Second to First changed to Central
  Avenue" and "Wolfskill Avenue | Central Avenue," both verbatim extents.
- ✔ **4th Street** — "Larkin Street | Fourth Street"; "Short Street from
  Fourth to Santa Fe Grounds | Fourth Street."
- ✔ **5th Street** — "Lugo Street | Fifth Street."
- ✔ **Marion Avenue** — "The name Marion Avenue restored."
- ✔ **Stanford Avenue / Crocker Street** — "The name Ruth Avenue restored."
- ⚠ **3rd Street, east of Mission Rd** — research-leads.md carries a HOT LEAD
  built on the Feb. 19 Herald's "The name of Mono street, which had been
  changed to Third, was restored." The corresponding line in the manuscript
  committee report reads **Moore**, not Mono, which makes it likely the
  Feb. 19 "Mono" is the same OCR slip. The lead should be treated as weak
  until a Mono Street is independently located; do not build a segment story
  on it.

## Items to bank (out of current coverage)

- **"Broadway Street" → "Canal Street"** (Toll, Feb. 23). ⚠ Almost certainly
  NOT downtown Broadway, which had been Fort Street until 1890 and is
  "Broadway" without the "Street." Locate the actual street before letting
  this near the Broadway entry.
- Stanley Avenue → Tropico Avenue; Dana → Stanley Avenue (Grider); Wood
  Avenue, Morrison Road to Soto → Griffin Avenue (Toll).
- The mayor's Feb. 8 list of 18 objected-to Spanish names — a ready-made
  namesake-research batch for whichever neighborhoods they land in.
- Context worth citing once a suitable entry exists: the commission had a
  bill introduced in Sacramento requiring that no subdivision be recorded
  until its street names and their connections to existing streets were
  approved by the city — the origin of the practice the tract-map pipeline
  now relies on.
