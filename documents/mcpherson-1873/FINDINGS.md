## 1. The three appendixes — answered

| | Printed pp. | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **No. 1** | 151–169 | ⭐⭐ **The land-and-streets retrospect.** Seventeen instruments, numbered "No. 1"–"No. 17" by the compiler, reaching back to **January 1852**. The preface: "Ordinances mainly pertaining to Land, Streets, Title to Land, and to Maps of Zanjas and Streets, **some of which have been repealed, but nevertheless deemed important**." This is where the oldest street material lives. |
| **No. 2** | 171–178 | State statutes authorising the levies for ten city funds, and the acts for liquidating five bond issues; plus Rules of the Police Department and a list of "Acts pertaining to city corporation—1850 to 1872". **No streets.** |
| **No. 3** | 179–197 | ⭐ **The 1872–73 supplement.** Seventeen more instruments, again numbered 1–17, running Sept. 1872 → **4 March 1873** — everything passed while the book was in press. Signed by Cristobal Aguilar, then J. R. Toberman. Contains the Dec. 1872 ordinance adopting eight official street maps. |

Each appendix has its own short index at the back (PDF 214, 215, 216), all three
transcribed in full in `mcpherson-1873-contents.md` §6.

**⚠ Appendix No. 1's numbering is not a sequence you can trust.** The printed
"No. n" headings are not chronological, some are missing or illegible, and the
appendix's own index cites a "No. 19" for a zanja on p. 159 — outside the 1–17
range. Cite by **appendix + number + printed page**, and prefer the *City
Archives Vol. VII, p. N* citation McPherson prints under each one: that points at
the original record, which is what an archive request needs.

## 2. Calle de los Negros — a hard, clean negative ⚠

**It is not in this book, under any name.** Searched the OCR of all 217 pages,
case-insensitively: `negro` 0 hits, `negros` 0, `calle de` 0, `calle` only inside
English words ("called"). No Negro Alley, no Los Angeles Alley, no Nigger Alley.

**How much weight this carries.** Some, but less than one would like:

- The book is a **compilation of ordinances that survived and were thought worth
  reprinting**. The preface says outright that "many of the original copies are
  lost." A street with no ordinance about it simply does not appear.
- Calle de los Negros was a **short private-frontage alley** running north from
  the Plaza between the Coronel and Requena properties. Nothing in the city's
  legislative business of 1852–73 needed to name it — it was neither opened,
  graded, widened nor renamed in that period.
- ⭐ **But the book comes within a few feet of it and looks away.** The 1854
  lines-and-names ordinance (p. 137) fixes the *eastern line of Los Angeles
  street* "from the house of Felipe Rhein to an angle in the house of **Manuel
  Requena** … thence to a stake planted by the Surveyor immediately in front of
  the house of **Soledad Yndart de Coronel**, and thence along the wall of the
  house of **Francis Mellus**." That is the Calle de los Negros frontage,
  surveyed house by house — and the survey names the *houses*, not the *alley*.
- The 1852 city-limits ordinance (p. 161) likewise walks the Plaza edge and names
  no alley there.

**So the finding is: in the city's own law, 1852–1873, Calle de los Negros had no
legal existence as a named street.** That is a real and citable result for the
switchover blocker — it explains why the corpus cannot place it, and it means the
placement will have to come from a *map* (Ord's survey, an early Sanborn, or the
1873 Plaza sheet already held as `documents/oldplaza1873`) and not from an
ordinance. ⚠ Recommend recording it as a negative with exactly this scope, not as
"absent from the 1873 record".

## 3. The Ord survey — disappointed, but not empty ⚠

**Ord's 1849 survey is not printed and its street names are not listed.** It is
*cited* four times and treated throughout as a document on file that every reader
could consult:

- p. 62 — lots in **block 42, Ord's survey**
- p. 67 — "lots Nos. 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 of **block No. 40, of Ord's survey**"; and
  "lots 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6, in **block 41, of Ord's survey**, and bounded on one
  side by **Hornet street**, on the other by **College and Bull streets**"
- p. 136 — the east line of Main street run to the south line of Fifth street
  "**according to Ord's survey**"
- p. 163 — "That **on the map of the city, Ord's survey**, the streets parallel
  to, and south of Eighth street, be numbered in succession, 9th, 10th, 11th and
  12th streets."

What that still buys the project:

1. **Ord's survey was the operative city map through 1857–1869**, by the city's
   own statement, and its blocks were numbered to at least 42 before the 1857
   ordinance extended the numbering to 89.
2. ⭐ **Block 41 of Ord's survey is bounded by Hornet, College and Bull streets**
   (p. 67). `hornet` is the entity `documents/ord-1874-02-26` renames to Yale
   under the council's spelling "Wasp"; here it is, on an Ord block, next to
   **Bull**, which the same 1874 ordinance renames to Castelar. **Two of the
   three streets of the Feb. 1874 renaming are located against Ord's own block
   numbering, five years earlier.**
3. **`documents/guinn-1895` remains the only account of Ord's names** the project
   holds. This volume does not displace it.

## 5. Bulk lists of street names — four, earliest 1852

Full transcriptions in `mcpherson-1873-street-ordinances.md` §C.

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **App. 1 No. 13, 29 June 1857** (p. 163) — the block-numbering ordinance.
   An **ordered, exhaustive roster of the downtown north–south streets**, because
   a block enumeration cannot skip one: **Main · Spring · Fort · Hill · Olive ·
   Charity · Hope · Flower · Grasshopper**, with **Pico** closing the grid below
   Twelfth and **Eighth** as the datum. It is simultaneously the **naming act for
   Ninth, Tenth, Eleventh and Twelfth streets**.
   *The project's earliest broad sources were an 1876 plat and an 1878
   compilation. This is 1857. It is the new floor.*
2. ⭐⭐ **Ch. IV §§55, 57, fire limits** (pp. 47–48) — nineteen names walked as two
   closed perimeters, each located by its neighbours. Includes **Bull**, **Short**,
   **Garcia**, **College**, **High**, **Jail**, **Lovers' Lane**.
3. ⭐⭐ **App. 3 No. 10, 6 Dec. 1872** (pp. 189–190) — eight official street maps,
   introducing **Requena street**, **Ogier Lane**, **Macy street**, **Old Aliso
   street** and **Canal and Reservoir street**.
4. ⭐ **Ch. XXV §285, 29 April 1864** (pp. 124–125) — the Childs zanja grant,
   bounding blocks by Grasshopper, Sixth, Flower, Seventh, Ninth, Twelfth,
   **New Main**, Charity, **Pio Pico**, Olive.

## 6. What surprised me

- **A renaming instrument, in full, from 1872.** I expected at best an index
  reference. §305 is the complete ordinance in one sentence with its approval
  date, sitting inside the revised code as an ordinary section.
- **The compiler prints repealed ordinances on purpose.** Twice he says so in a
  signed note — "it has been thought best to give them all, that it may be easily
  determined whether any vested rights have accrued under the ordinances so
  repealed" (p. 135), and the same for the three First Street ordinances (p. 134).
  **The book is a stratigraphy, not a snapshot.** New High Street appears five
  times over eighteen years. Most compilations print only what is in force.
- **The index does work the text does not.** Two of the four renamings exist
  *only* as index glosses (Bridge→Turner, the alley→Arcadia). McPherson knew
  things about his own streets that he did not put in the body.
- **1852 has a street called Principal.** *Calle Principal* survives into an
  English-language city ordinance, and **Eighth Street already exists in 1852** —
  five years before the ordinance that numbered Ninth to Twelfth from it. I had
  assumed the numbered grid was later.
- **Mathew Teed, in a city ordinance, on Buenavista Street, 1870** (p. 73). The
  project carries `teed-street` as `inferred`.
- **"Figaroa" and "Figueroa" in the same book**, in two printings of the *same*
  boundary description (pp. 68 and 120).
- **"Chavis streets"** at Alameda, March 1873 (p. 196), eye-checked — a pre-1873
  spelling of Chavez.
- **Blocks 35 and 36 of Hancock's survey** turn up at p. 143 in an ordinance
  opening an unnamed street west to Grasshopper. `documents/mr003-240` is the map
  of Block 35½. Worth an hour.

## 8. What needs your decision

1. ⚠⚠ **The Franklin anachronism, and how much of this book it puts in doubt.**
   The 1854 ordinance as McPherson prints it (p. 137) says Manuel Requena's house
   fronted **Franklin street**. Franklin did not exist until Jail was renamed on
   3 April 1872 — by §305 of this same book, eighteen years later. Either:
   (a) the compiler silently modernised a street name inside text he introduced as
   given "in full", which would mean **no street name anywhere in this volume can
   be trusted to be contemporary with its instrument**; or
   (b) there was an earlier, unrelated Franklin street near the Plaza.
   The archive citation is printed — **Archives, Vol. VII, pp. 419–420** — so this
   is settleable by one document pull, and it should be settled before any row is
   built on a pre-1872 instrument in this book.

2. **How old-series instruments get identified.** This volume is the hardest case
   in the corpus because it offers three incompatible handles at once
   (revised-code section, appendix running number, City Archives volume+page).
   ⭐ **Recommendation: make the City Archives citation the identity** —
   `Archives Vol. VII p. 419` is stable, points past this book at the original,
   and is what a researcher would actually request. The section and appendix
   numbers are properties of *this compilation* and will collide with Caswell
   1878's and Robinson 1884's.
   ⚠ I have created no `ord-*` or `sec-*` folder and written no rows.

3. **Whether an index gloss may date a renaming.** Bridge→Turner and alley→Arcadia
   are assertions by a named compiler in an official publication, but they are not
   instruments and carry no date. `handbook/RENAMING-SOURCES.md` step 3 would
   exclude them. ⭐ They are strong enough to *bracket* a change and to justify a
   `spellings` entry, but not to carry a `change` row. Your call.

4. **Whether Jail → Franklin should be entered now.** It is complete, dated and
   unqualified, and it is the earliest renaming instrument the project has. It is
   held only by decision 2. ⭐ If the identification scheme will take a while, this
   one is worth an exception.

5. **`handbook/COMPILED-ORDINANCES.md` needs a fourth row.** Its table lists
   Caswell 1878, Robinson 1884 and Teed 1887 as "the compiled-ordinance volumes".
   McPherson 1873 is now the earliest of them, it is the only one the project holds
   *in full* rather than as an index, and its Appendix No. 1 reaches back to 1852.
   The file's ⚠ note — "These are indexes only. Every page reference below is a
   page we have NOT yet read" — no longer covers the series.
   ⚠ I did not edit that file.

6. ⭐ **Two concrete archive requests this volume generates**, both cheap:
   - **City Surveyor's maps Nos. 62–69**, adopted 6 Dec. 1872 (App. 3 No. 10,
     p. 190) — including the Macy street extension and the Short street /
     Canal and Reservoir street continuation.
   - **City Archives Vol. VII**, cited by McPherson at pp. 100, 196, 331, 419–420,
     477, 479, 483, 493, 496 and 607. Ten located leaves of the original 1850s–60s
     ordinance record.
