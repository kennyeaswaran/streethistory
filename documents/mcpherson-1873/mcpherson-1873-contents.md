## 2. There is no table of contents

✔ The book has no front table of contents. Its place is taken by three things:

1. **A section list at the head of each chapter** — two columns, "SECTION——. |
   SECTION——.", listing every section number in that chapter with a caption. The
   Streets chapter's list (Ch. XXVI, printed pp. 127–128) is the single most
   useful page in the book and is transcribed in full in §5 below.
2. **The back-of-book indexes** — Index to Charter, Index to Ordinances, and a
   separate short index to each of the three appendixes (§6).
3. **The preface** (✔ transcribed in §3), which is the only place that says what
   the appendixes are for.

## 3. Preface — verbatim ✔ (PDF 3)

> **PREFACE.**
>
> Whenever the phrase, "Approved June 1st, 1869," occurs, it means that the
> sections preceding it, up to the commencement of the chapter, or to the next
> preceding date of approval, are taken from the pamphlet, "Revised Ordinances of
> the City of Los Angeles, passed and approved June 1st, 1869," and not the date
> of their original approval by the Common Council and Mayor, (as many of the
> original copies are lost.) All other sections or ordinances marked approved on
> a different date, are the actual dates of approval by the municipal
> authorities. The foot notes to the Charter call attention to amendments and
> construction by the Supreme Court to the sections preceding. Appendix No. 1,
> contains Ordinances mainly pertaining to Land, Streets, Title to Land, and to
> Maps of Zanjas and Streets, some of which have been repealed, but nevertheless
> deemed important.
>
> Appexdix No. 2 contains the reference to the Statutes, and quotes the sections
> authorizing and directing the Council to make levies for the various Funds. It
> also, in like manner, makes reference to the Statutes, and quotes the sections
> thereof, directing how the various Debts created under them may be gradually
> liquidated. This Appendix will be found to be essentially convenient for the
> municipal authorities; to all persons desiring to readily acquaint themselves
> with the condition of the Public Debt of the city, and the process of its
> liquidation.
>
> W. McPHERSON,
> Compiler,
> Attorney at law—Office, No. 55, Temple Block.

("Appexdix" is the printer's error, ✔ on the page. The preface says nothing at
all about Appendix No. 3 — see §4.)

⚠⚠ **The dating rule in the first sentence is load-bearing for this project.**
A section in the body marked "Approved June 1st, 1869" is dated to the *1869
re-enactment*, **not** to the original act, "as many of the original copies are
lost." So a section so marked gives a *by* date, never an *on* date. Sections
carrying any other date carry the real date of approval.

## 4. The three appendixes — what they actually are

### Appendix No. 1 — printed pp. 151–169 (PDF 152–170) ⭐⭐ THE STREET APPENDIX

✔ Titled `APPENDIX No. 1` at printed p. 151. Contains **seventeen numbered
instruments, "No. 1" through "No. 17"**, each given as "An Ordinance …" with its
approval date and a citation to *City Archives* or *City Records*, volume and
page. The numbers are **McPherson's own running numbers inside the appendix**,
not city ordinance numbers — Los Angeles did not number its ordinances until
1883. They are **not in chronological order** and the sequence has gaps in the
printed headings.

The preface calls it "Ordinances mainly pertaining to Land, Streets, Title to
Land, and to Maps of Zanjas and Streets, some of which have been repealed, but
nevertheless deemed important." That is accurate: it is a deliberate retrospect,
reaching back to **1852**, and it is where the oldest street material in the
volume sits.

Its own index (✔ PDF 214, §6c below) lists under **Streets**: Short, Alameda,
Los Angeles, Commercial, **Arcadia**, "numbering certain", and New High.

### Appendix No. 2 — printed pp. 171–178 (PDF 172–179)

✔ "Special Statutes under which the Mayor and Common Council…". State acts
authorizing levies for the Loan Fund of 1862, Interest and Sinking Fund of 1862,
River Fund, Street Fund, Gas Fund, Railroad Fund, Direct Tax, Interest and
Sinking Fund of 1870, Water Fund, School Fund; then acts for the liquidation of
the bonds of 1862 (improvements), 1862 (funded debt), S.P.R.R. Co. 1868, 1870
(funded debt), 1872 (school house); Rules of the Police Department (p. 165);
"Acts pertaining to city corporation—1850 to 1872" (p. 168).
**Nothing about streets.** Money and debt only, exactly as the preface says.

⚠ Its index cites pp. 165 and 168 — *before* the appendix's own first page 171 —
so the Rules of Police and the Acts list are physically bound into the end of
Appendix No. 1's run and indexed with Appendix No. 2. ⚠ OCR-level observation,
not eye-checked page by page.

### Appendix No. 3 — printed pp. 179–197 (PDF 180–198) ⭐ THE 1872–73 SUPPLEMENT

✔ Titled `APPENDIX No. 3` at printed p. 179 (OCR misreads the 3 as B).
The preface never mentions it. It is a **supplement of the newest ordinances**,
seventeen of them again numbered "No. 1" to "No. 17", running from
**Sept./Oct. 1872 to March 4, 1873** — i.e. everything passed while the book was
being compiled, ending weeks before publication. Mayors signing: Cristobal
Aguilar, then J. R. Toberman.

Its index (✔ PDF 216) has three street entries: "Records, certain maps declared"
(No. 10, p. 189), "Streets, sidewalks, etc., amending ordinance concerning"
(No. 14, p. 193), "Street, Mission, lines of" (No. 17, p. 196).

⭐ **Appendix No. 3, No. 10 (p. 189–190) is the richest single page in the book
for street names** — see the shortlist.
