# McPherson 1873 — the street shortlist

Everything in this volume that **names, renames, vacates, opens, extends,
widens, grades or defines the lines of a street**, or that **lists street names
in bulk**. Ordered by value to the project, not by page.

**How to cite an instrument in this book.** ⚠ This volume predates ordinance
numbering entirely (Los Angeles first numbered an ordinance in Nov. 1883,
No. 104). The book offers exactly three citation handles, and they do not
interchange:

1. **Part Second, Chapter N, Section S** — the revised code. The section numbers
   run 1–366 straight through all thirty-five chapters, so a section number alone
   is unique. Streets are Ch. XXVI, §§292–348.
2. **Appendix No. 1, No. n** and **Appendix No. 3, No. n** — McPherson's own
   running numbers inside each appendix, 1–17 in both. ⚠ *Appendix No. 1, No. 13*
   and *Appendix No. 3, No. 13* are different instruments. The appendix must
   always be named.
3. **City Archives / City Records, Vol. VII, p. N** — the archive citation
   McPherson prints under each appendix ordinance. This is the only handle that
   points at the original record rather than at this book.

Rows marked ✔ were read off the page image; ⚠ OCR rows were not.

## ⭐⭐ C. Bulk lists of street names

The project's brief asks for "any list of street names, in any form." There are
**four** in this volume, and the earliest is 1852.

### C1. ⭐⭐⭐ App. 1, No. 13 — "An Ordinance Numbering Certain Streets and Blocks", approved **June 29, 1857**, printed p. 163 (PDF 164) ✔

Verbatim, complete:

> **An Ordinance Numbering Certain Streets and Blocks.**
> Be it ordained by the Mayor and Common Council of the City of Los Angeles as
> follows:
> That **on the map of the city, Ord's survey**, the streets parallel to, and
> south of Eighth street, be numbered in succession, **9th, 10th, 11th and 12th
> streets**. That the blocks lying south of 8th street be numbered in the
> following manner:
> Block bounded by 8, 9, **Main and Spring** streets be block No. 50; by 8, 9,
> **Spring and Fort**, 51; by 8, 9, **Fort and Hill**, 52; by 8, 9, **Hill and
> Olive**, 53; by 8, 9, **Olive and Charity**, 54; by 8, 9, **Charity and
> Hope**, 55; by 8, 9, **Hope and Flower**, 56; by 8, 9, **Flower and
> Grasshopper**, 57; by 9, 10, Main and Spring, 58; by 9, 10, Spring and Fort,
> 59; by 9, 10, Fort and Hill, 60; by 9, 10, Hill and Olive, 61; by 9, 10, Olive
> and Charity, 62; by 9, 10, Charity and Hope, 63; by 9, 10, Hope and Flower,
> 64; by 9, 10, Flower and Grasshopper, 65; by 10, 11, Main and Spring, 66; by
> 10, 11, Spring and Fort, 67; by 10, 11, Fort and Hill, 68; by 10, 11, Hill and
> Olive, 69; by 10, 11, Olive and Charity, 70; by 10, 11, Charity and Hope, 71;
> by 10, 11, Hope and Flower, 72; by 10, 11, Flower and Grasshopper, 73; by 11,
> 12, Main and Spring, 74; by 11, 12, Spring and Fort, 75; by 11, 12, Fort and
> Hill, 76; by 11, 12, Hill and Olive, 77. The block bounded by 11, 12, Olive and
> Charity, 78; Charity and Hope, 79; Hope and Flower, 80; Flower and
> Grasshopper, 81. The fractional blocks, bounded by 12, **Pico**, Main and
> Spring, 82; 12, Pico, Spring and Fort, 83; 12, Pico, Fort and Hill, 84; 12,
> Pico, Hill and Olive, 85; 12, Pico, Olive and Charity, 86; 12, Pico, Charity
> and Hope, 87; 12, Pico, Hope and Flower, 88; 12, Pico, Flower and Grasshopper,
> 89.
> Approved June 29, 1857.
> A. F. CORONEL, President. W. G. DRYDEN, Clerk. JOHN G. NICHOLS, Mayor.
> **City Archives—Vol. VII, P. 477.**

**Why this is the find of the volume.**

- It is **the naming act for Ninth, Tenth, Eleventh and Twelfth Streets**, dated
  to the day: June 29, 1857.
- It is **an ordered, exhaustive west-to-east roster of the downtown north–south
  streets as of 1857**, and the order is unambiguous because it is a block
  enumeration: **Main · Spring · Fort · Hill · Olive · Charity · Hope · Flower ·
  Grasshopper**, with **Pico** closing the grid on the south below Twelfth.
  Nine names, in sequence, nineteen years before the 1876 plat.
- It cites "**the map of the city, Ord's survey**" as the instrument the numbering
  is laid on. ⚠ It does **not** print Ord's survey or its names — see FINDINGS §3.
- It confirms **Charity Street** running the full depth from Eighth to Pico in
  1857, which bears directly on the unresolved question in
  `handbook/COMPILED-ORDINANCES.md` about whether Ord. 286 (Charity → Grand)
  renamed the whole length.
- It confirms **Grasshopper Street** (later Pearl, later Figueroa) as the western
  edge of the grid in 1857, seventeen years before the Feb. 1874 renaming.
- **Eighth street already exists** in 1857 and is the datum the others are
  numbered from.

⭐ **Nineteen street names in two paragraphs, each located by its neighbours**:
Fourth, Fort, San Pedro, First, Alameda, Sansevaine, Garcia, Lovers' Lane,

⭐⭐ **Bull street is here**, placed exactly: it runs south from College to Short.
The Bull → Castelar ordinance is the hunt `handbook/RENAMING-SOURCES.md` was
written for; this gives Bull Street a hard 1869/1873 extent.
⭐ **Short street is here too**, running east from Bull to New High — which with
App. 3 No. 10 (row A4) places the whole Short/Canal-and-Reservoir/Bellevue line.

### C5. ⚠ App. 1, No. 17 — city limits of **Jan. 22, 1852**, printed pp. 160–161 (PDF 161–162) ✔ (p. 161)
