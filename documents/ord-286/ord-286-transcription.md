Transcribed by eye, 2026-09-19, from a contrast-normalised 200 dpi render of

ORDINANCE No. 286.

An Ordinance changing the name of Charity street to Grand avenue.

The Mayor and Council of the City of Los Angeles, do ordain as follows:

Section 1. That the name of that certain street in the City of Los Angeles,
heretofore known as Charity street, be changed, and that hereupon and
hereafter the same be known and designated as Grand avenue.

Sec. 2. All ordinances or parts of ordinances in conflict with this ordinance
are hereby repealed.

Sec. 3. The Clerk of the Council shall certify to the passage of this
ordinance, and shall cause the same to be published once in the Los Angeles
Daily TRIBUNE, and thereupon and thereafter it shall take effect and be in
force.

I hereby certify that the foregoing ordinance was adopted by the Council of
the City of Los Angeles, at its meeting of February 15, 1887.

FREEMAN G. TEED,
Clerk of the Council of the City of Los Angeles.

Approved this 21st day of February, 1887.

L. N. BREED,
Mayor pro tem.

Published February 22, 1887.

## Notes on the reading

- ⭐⭐⭐ **THERE IS NO EXTENT CLAUSE.** Section 1 is the whole of the operative
  text and it quantifies over the NAME — "the name of that certain street …
  heretofore known as Charity street" — not over a stretch of it. The
  ordinance states no from, no to, no tract, no portion. Whatever ground bore
  the name Charity Street on 22 February 1887 became Grand Avenue.
- The publication line on printed p. 249 reads **"Published February 22, 1837."**
  — a printer's slip for 1887. The certification and approval on the same page
  both read 1887, and the ordinances on either side (Nos. 285 and 287) are
  February and April 1887. Transcribed above as 1887 with the slip recorded
  here.
- Ordinance No. 285, immediately above on the same page, was adopted at the same
  meeting of February 15, 1887, approved the same 21st of February, and
  published the same 22nd — so the whole leaf is one council day.
- **L. N. Breed signs as Mayor pro tem**, not W. H. Workman. Workman signs
  ordinances from No. 275 (January 1887) onward; Breed signs Nos. 285 and 286.
- The publishing paper is the **Los Angeles Daily Tribune**, not the Herald.
  The Herald was the city's paper of record for ordinances up to about
  No. 262 and the Tribune after; handbook/RENAMING-SOURCES.md §2b.

## What the rest of the volume says about the same street

- **Ord. No. 156** (p. 63) and **Ord. No. 188** (p. 103) both establish the
  lines of **Charity street from a point 84 feet north of Pico street to
  Jefferson street** — a described strip 7,590 feet long, from Feldhauser's
  subdivision of block 86 of Ord's survey down to block 2 of Hancock's survey
  of 35-acre lots.
- The GENERAL INDEX (printed p. 642) gives Charity street grade entries at
  **Court House street** (Res. 436), **from Seventh street to Ninth street**
  (Res. 387), **at Pico street** (Res. 423 a) and **at Washington street**
  (Res. 488) — so the name ran continuously from the Court House block down
  past Washington.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Resolution No. 488**, printed p. 579, establishing the grade of
  Washington street from Main street to Figueroa street, reads: *"At the
  intersection of **Grand avenue (Charity street)**, the grade shall be 42.00
  feet below the datum plane."* The city glossed the new name with the old, at
  Washington Street. ⚠ That resolution was adopted **February 1, 1887**, two
  weeks BEFORE this ordinance — see documents/teed-1887/FINDINGS.md §2.
- **Ord. No. 306** (p. 280), adopted April 25, 1887, defines the lines of Grand
  avenue by reference to a map filed with the Clerk April 18, 1887 and entitled
  *"a plat showing extension and opening of Charity street"* — two months after
  the renaming, the plat was still the Charity street plat. That map is not in
  this corpus.
