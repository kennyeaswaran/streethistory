# What to look for in person

A trip sheet: things this project cannot get at from a keyboard, with the call
numbers and access facts needed on the day. Written to be opened on a phone at a
desk in a library. Each item says what it would settle, so a trip that gets
through only half the list still pays.

**Before writing anything down here, list the folder.** A lead added to this file
on 2026-09-17 named five title blocks as a trip; four of the sheets were already
in `documents/` and were read that afternoon in ten minutes (TRACT-RESEARCH.md,
"Before you plan a trip"). Searches that need only a browser, not a building,
are in **handbook/WANTED.md**. How to get into UCLA, UCI and the other libraries
in general, and which databases each licenses, is in **handbook/NAME-RESEARCH.md**,
"Libraries and licensed databases".

## 1. UCLA, Young Research Library — the Violé 1904 map ⭐ TOP PRIORITY

> **Map of Los Angeles / compiled & drawn by Félix Violé for the Chamber of
> Commerce.** 1904, ©1903. OCLC 25268136.
> **YRL — A-Level Map Cases — `G4364 Los Angeles P2 ns Violé 1904`**
> (a second copy, LSC Special Collections Backlog `SCB 135673 LOCIM`, is the
> one to ignore)

**Access.** The A-Level map cases are **open-stack, self-service**: no
appointment, no paging slip, no Special Collections registration. It needs only
an open YRL, so a Saturday will do. Check hours before setting out:
https://calendar.library.ucla.edu/hours. MapCallFinder gives the drawer's
call-number range: https://guides.library.ucla.edu/maps/locating. Map librarian:
**Maggie Tarmey, mtarmey@library.ucla.edu**. Not digitised anywhere found — not
LOC, LAPL, Rumsey or Calisphere.

**Why it matters.** It is the copy George Garrigues worked from ("you can find
the map in the UCLA map room"), and a complete snapshot of the names in force in
1903, in the middle of the period the corpus covers. Garrigues gives only the
names that had changed by 2002; the map answers what his list cannot. Compare it
with Stevenson's 1884 map, already in the corpus as `documents/stevenson-1884-hi-res`.

**What to shoot, in this order.**

1. **The street index on the verso, whole, before anything else.** Dealer
   descriptions of this folding pocket map (~21¼ × 24 in., engraved by Thorpe)
   say it carries an alphabetised street index on the back. That is probably
   what Garrigues transcribed. Column by column, overlapping, legible.
2. **The whole map, in overlapping tiles**, enough to stitch.
3. **These questions**, if there is time at the drawer. Most can be answered
   later from the index and the tiles.

| # | Look for | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mill Street** — present or absent? | If present, Mill predates the 1904 Industrial Tract and the "Molino (1896) came first, Mill is the echo" reading in `mill-st-arts-district` collapses. The cleanest single question on the sheet. |
| 2 | **Lemon Street and Wilson Street** — both there, and where? | The biggest open inference in the corpus: Lemon renamed Wilson in 1887 (our reading), or a separate neighbouring street (Kines's). `lemon-st-arts-district`. |
| 3 | **Orange Street**, Figueroa to Alvarado | 1903 sits between the ordinances, so it should read ORANGE; the change row `orange-st-wilshire` → `wilshire` wants an extent. |
| 4 | **Alabama Street** | Gone by 1903; we do not know what it became. The block bounded by W. 1st, Court, Colton and Temple (M.R. 5-69, the 1883 state-name sheet). |
| 5 | **Everett St / Everett Pl**, **St. Paul Ave / St. Paul Pl** | One entity each here, two streets each in the city registry. St. Paul Place is unminted and waiting on this. |
| 6 | **Stevenson Avenue** — is there one, and where? | `stevenson-ave` has no row. Look for it on Stevenson's own 1884 map as well. |
| 7 | **Georgia Street / Georgia Bell** | Georgia's coverage is about 10%, the worst of the hard coverage items. |
| 8 | **Mimosa Street** — present, and its extent | `mimosa-st` claims Tract No. 1687 (1912) did not coin it. |
| 9 | **Ida Street** — how many? | Two entities; Garrigues implies a third near Old Temple Road and Marathon. |
| 10 | **Market Street** off the Temple corridor | `market-st` is deliberately rowless; its internal note names this map as one of the two things that would unpark it. |
| 11 | Anything around **Boylston and Bixel** | The 1884 gemstone sheet's ground (M.R. 6-115). |

4. **If there is time:** anything in the cases catalogued to **Félix Violé** or
   the Chamber of Commerce for adjacent years. A second edition brackets changes
   instead of snapshotting them.

**Also at UCLA, on campus wifi only:** **Ancestry Library Edition** (US city
directories 1822–1995; UCI does not license it) —
https://guides.library.ucla.edu/c.php?g=1133156&p=8521627. Run the directory
lookups in §2 from there if LAPL is not on the trip. (The same database is free
with an Orange County Public Library card.)

**No longer UCLA errands:** the compiled-ordinance volumes (all five are in the
corpus, from Gale) and Stevenson's 1884 map (the Huntington scan is in the
corpus; UCLA's copy is a 1966 reproduction). The ordinances the compiled volumes
do not reach — 3829, 3847, 4093 (1896–97) and 16790, 16915, 16916, 48435 (1908,
1924) — are a City Clerk / City Archivist request, tracked in WANTED.md.

## 2. LAPL Central, History & Genealogy

### Bernice Kimball, *Street Names of Los Angeles* (Bureau of Engineering, 1988)

Not online anywhere; LAPL holds it. The Bureau's own compendium of name changes,
compiled from city records; Kines relies on it. Every entity below currently
says its date is not documented.

| Look up | What it would settle |
|---|---|
| **Colton Street** | when State Street became Colton (we guess the 1897 commission) |
| **Delong Street** | when Virginia became De Long, and who De Long was |
| **Fremont Avenue** | when Beaudry Street became Fremont — the Oct. 1890 ordinance was vetoed, so the real change is undated |
| **Hewitt Street** | who Hewitt was; searched hard, never found |
| **Toluca Street** | corroborate the Oct. 1888 duplicate cull |
| **Huntley Drive** | when Sapphire Street became Huntley |
| **Margo Street** | when Palm Street became Margo |
| **Firman / Firmin Street** | which spelling is right, and when Fireman changed |
| **Imperial Street** | the Palm → Mimosa → Imperial sequence |
| **Weller Street** | who Weller was; whether the Onizuka renaming was 1987 or 1988 |
| **Market Street / West Market Street** | West Market has no entity; we know it only from Guinn |
| **Witmer Street** | three 1885–87 tract names (Bryan, Nichols, Logan) lie on this line and the Oct. 1888 committee wanted NICHOLS; Witmer won, and nothing says when or why |
| **Garland Avenue** | confirm Kines's 1897 date for Holmes → Garland against the Bureau's own record |
| **Ingraham Street** | who Ingraham was — a firm three-way negative online |
| **Anderson Street** | who Anderson was, and what became of the Anderson Avenue folded into it in Oct. 1888 |
| **Loma Drive** | when Belmont Avenue became Loma Drive — the sheets bracket it, nothing dates it |

### City directories, 1873–1907

Online at LAPL's ResCarta, but it is a JavaScript app, so a person or a browser
(SERIAL-SOURCES.md). Also on Ancestry Library Edition (§1).

- ⭐ **1886–87, "Lemon Street."** Kines says D. C. Wilson was living on it. The
  entry places Lemon and settles the Lemon/Wilson question (§1, item 2).
- **A. Weill** — expand the initial to a full name. See §4.

## 3. The Huntington — Solano-Reeve survey collection (`mssSolano`)

Surveyors' manuscript maps. Item-level metadata is not exposed online; the
reading room has it. Two of the best-attested naming sets in the corpus have
untraced subdividers, and these sheets are the likeliest place their names are
written down. Ask for:

- Hancock's Survey, **Block 35** — the 1883 state-name sheet (Alabama, Ohio,
  Virginia, State). The online listing shows Blocks 17, 22, 24–25, 36–37 and B
  but no Block 35; ask whether one exists.
- Hancock's Survey, **Block 38** — the 1884 gemstone sheet (Diamond, Emerald,
  Jewel, Ruby, Sapphire, South Diamond). Catalogued item: *"[Los Angeles: partial
  street plan, showing part of Ord's Survey and Blocks 36-39, Hancock's Survey,
  with surface detail]"*.
- The **Alanis Vineyard Tract** — catalogued as running "Alameda to Vignes St.,
  Sainsevain St. to Labory Lane", surveyed "at the request of I.W. Hellmann and
  Chas. Ducommun". Worth seeing whole: it georeferences the Frenchtown cluster
  and may name more of the circle.
- Anything for the **Harvey Tract** (M.R. 10-77, 1886), which would give Adele a
  family, and **Tract No. 320** (1908), which would give Nina one.

## 4. The A. Weill tract ⭐⭐

`weill` has no first name, an `extensive` empty search behind it, and a confirmed
zero in Newmark. The Oct. 1888 committee names an **"A. Weill tract"** with a
Cedar Street in it, so the man filed a subdivision and its title block has his
full name. Stevenson's 1884 map places **A. WEILL, 56.64 acres**, south-east of
downtown, between O. J. Mairs, J. Philbin, Goetz and H. Niemaier.

- **The filing:** NavigateLA or the County map books (a browser, or the
  Recorder's books in person). It is a sibling of the sheet Weill Street sits
  on, not that sheet.
- **The name:** a city directory (§2).

## 5. Small lookups to carry on any trip

None needs a particular library — a city directory, a census, a deed index or one
good search — but they are easy to finish at a desk with the sources to hand.

- **Seven people from the title blocks** (the sheets are in `documents/`). Any one
  who turns out to be the tract's principal settles a cluster of names at once:
  **B. E. Day** (Clement Tract, M.R. 17-41) · **E. W. Jones** (Kennedy Tract,
  M.R. 6-193) · **E. Bouton** (Truman Tract, M.R. 5-405) · **W. A. Boring**
  (possibly the architect William A. Boring — untested), **N. W. Stowell**,
  **Mrs M. A. Hoadley**, **W. J. Bradshaw** (all Colina Park, M.R. 7-36).
- **Matthew Teed's land.** Nothing yet connects him to Block 35½ beyond the
  coincidence that he sat on the Council (1874–76) when the Teed Street sheet was
  recorded in July 1876. A deed, an assessment roll, or Robinson 1884's Ord. 107
  ("Ordering sale of lots in block 35½, Ord's survey", p. 335, in
  `documents/robinson-1884`) would test it. ⚠ Any "Teed" in a city record needs
  sorting from **Freeman G. Teed** (1851–1916), city auditor 1886–88 and the
  compiler of the 1887 and 1889 ordinance volumes.

## 6. Never tried — whole sources this project has not touched

- **ProQuest Historical Newspapers: *Los Angeles Times*, 1881– .** An entire second
  newspaper; everything so far is the *Herald* (CDNC) and the *Express*. UCI
  licenses it, so it is a home resource with a UCI login; also via LAPL.
