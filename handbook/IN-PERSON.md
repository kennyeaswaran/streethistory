## 1b. UCLA — the compiled ordinance volumes ⚠ NEW, AND AWKWARDLY PLACED

**Kenny asked, 2026-09-18, whether UCLA might have the ordinance books in
print.** Answer: **there is no series that prints every ordinance by number** —
the City Clerk's ordinance books remain the only route to 16790 / 16915 / 16916 /
48435 and to 3829 / 3847 / 4093. But there IS a published series of *compiled*
ordinances, it was published by order of the Council, and **UCLA has the whole
early run**:

| Volume | Compiler | UCLA copies |
|---|---|---|
| **1878** Revised charter and compiled ordinances | Wm. M. Caswell | LAW `KFX1621 .A6 1878` · SRLF→LSC `JS1001 .A8 1878` · **also as a licensed e-book** |
| **1884** Compiled ordinances and resolutions | W. W. Robinson | LAW `KFX1621 .A6 1884` · SRLF→LSC `JS1001.A9 A3 1884` |
| **1887** Compiled ordinances and resolutions, **Vol. III** | Freeman G. Teed | SRLF→LSC `JS1001.A9 A3 1887` · **also as a licensed e-book** |
| **1889** Charter and compiled ordinances, **Vol. IV** | Freeman G. Teed | LAW `KFX1621 .A6 1889` · SRLF→LSC `JS1001 .A8 1889` |

⭐ **WHY THIS IS WORTH A DAY.** These are compilations of the ordinances *in
force*, indexed, covering exactly 1850–1889 — **the corpus's densest period and
the one where this project keeps having to infer dates it cannot document.** A
compiled volume of that era normally carries a streets chapter: opening,
grading, widening, vacating, naming. If the Feb. 1874 renaming ordinance, or
anything from the 1870s–80s street-name changes, was still in force at
compilation, its text is in one of these four books. **Nobody on this project has
ever looked.**

⚠ **THE CATCH IS ACCESS, and it is annoying.** Every volume is in one of two
places, and both are awkward:
- **Law Library** (`KFX…`, "Superseded Section") — open Saturdays, but closed to
  the public by policy. Worth an email to the Law Library first if a weekday is
  impossible; do not just turn up.
- **SRLF → "Non-Circ Request at UCLA YRL Special Collections"** (`JS…`) — these
  are off-site AND non-circulating, so they need an **LSC request at least two
  business days ahead**, and LSC is a **weekday-only** reading room. Register the
  LSC account first (speccoll.library.ucla.edu), then request all four at once.

⭐ **THE CHEAP SHOT FIRST:** the 1878 and 1887 volumes exist as **UCLA-licensed
electronic books** (no holdings attached, "1 online resource"; the OCLC numbers
sit in the range Gale uses for *The Making of Modern Law*). If that is right,
they open **in a browser on the UCLA network** with no reading room, no request
and no waiting. ⚠ Unverified — check by opening the record in UC Library Search
while on campus. If it works, two of the four volumes cost nothing.

⚠ And there is **no LA City Council minute run at UCLA at all** — print or film.
The nearest thing is *Chronological record of Los Angeles city officials,
1850–1938, compiled from the minutes of the City Council* (SRLF→LSC,
`JS1001 .A4 1966`), which is a roster, not proceedings.

## 2. LAPL Central, History & Genealogy — Bernice Kimball

**What:** **Bernice Kimball, *Street Names of Los Angeles*** (Bureau of
Engineering, 1988). Not online anywhere. LAPL holds it.

**Why:** it is the Bureau of Engineering's own compendium of name changes,
compiled from city records — i.e. the thing that would date the renamings this
project keeps having to infer. Kines relies on it.

**What to look up, in priority order** (all of these are entities that
currently say the date is not documented):

|---|---|
| **Colton Street** | when State Street became Colton (we guess the 1897 commission) |
| **Delong Street** | when Virginia became De Long, and who De Long was |
| **Fremont Avenue** | when Beaudry Street became Fremont — the Oct. 1890 ordinance was *vetoed*, so the real change is undated |
| **Hewitt Street** | who Hewitt was. Searched hard, never found |
| **Toluca Street** | corroborate the Oct. 1888 duplicate cull |
| **Huntley Drive** | when Sapphire Street became Huntley |
| **Margo Street** | when Palm Street became Margo |
| **Firman / Firmin Street** | which spelling is right, and when Fireman changed |

⭐ **TWO MEN THIS PROJECT KNOWS ONLY BY AN INITIAL**, added 2026-09-17, and a

- ✔✔ **M. TEED — CLOSED 2026-09-18, AND NOT IN A LIBRARY. He is MATTHEW
  TEED** (b. Budleigh, Devonshire, 17 Apr. 1828 — d. Los Angeles, 31 Mar. 1904),
  an English-born carpenter who learned the trade in San Francisco, lost a
  Stockton shop to fire in 1857, and sat on the **Common Council for three
  terms — 1870–73, 1874–76 and 1880–88**. He built a house on Fort Hill at 513
  California Street, served on the city's first Park Commission from 1895, and
  was a charter member of the Los Angeles Pioneers Association. Source: the
  Wikipedia article *Matthew Teed*, itself sourced to the **Chronological Record
  of Los Angeles City Officials (1938)** and his *Los Angeles Times* obituary of
  1 Apr. 1904. ⭐ **The 1874–76 term is the one that matters**: Teed Street is
  lettered on the Block 35½ sheet recorded **July 1876**, i.e. he was sitting on
  the Council at the moment the ground was platted.
  ⚠ **AND THERE IS A SECOND TEED IN CITY GOVERNMENT** — **Freeman G. Teed**
  (1851–1916), of Wilkes-Barre PA, city auditor 1886–88 and councilman 1892–96,
  who is the man who compiled the 1887 and 1889 ordinance volumes in §1b. He
  arrived too late to be the 1876 namesake, so this does not disturb the
  identification — but any future "Teed" hit in a city record needs sorting
  between the two. Whether they were related is unknown.
  ⚠ Still open, and now cheap: Matthew Teed's own **land**. Nothing yet connects
  him to the ground on Block 35½ beyond the coincidence of dates. ★ THE LESSON,
  again: this had been written down as a trip for the **Common Council rolls**
  and the **Old Settlers' Society list**, and it took one web search.
- **A. WEILL** — see §3a. A directory would give the name; the map book would
  give the tract.

## 3. The Huntington — Solano-Reeve survey collection

**What:** the **Solano-Reeve collection** of surveyors' manuscript maps
(`mssSolano`). Item-level metadata is not exposed online; the reading room has
it.

**Why:** two of the best-attested naming sets in the corpus have untraced
subdividers, and the Solano-Reeve sheets for Hancock's Survey are the likeliest
place their names are written down.

**What to ask for:**

- Hancock's Survey, **Block 35** — the 1883 state-name sheet (Alabama, Ohio,
  Virginia, State). The Huntington's online listing has Blocks 17, 22, 24–25,
  36–37 and B, but **no Block 35** is exposed; ask whether one exists.
- Hancock's Survey, **Block 38** — the 1884 gemstone sheet (Diamond, Emerald,
  Jewel, Ruby, Sapphire, South Diamond). There is a catalogued item,
  *"[Los Angeles: partial street plan, showing part of Ord's Survey and Blocks
  36-39, Hancock's Survey, with surface detail]"*, which covers Block 38.
- The **Alanis Vineyard Tract** sheet — catalogued as running *"Alameda to
  Vignes St., Sainsevain St. to Labory Lane"*, surveyed *"at the request of
  I.W. Hellmann and Chas. Ducommun"*. Worth seeing whole: it georeferences the
  whole Frenchtown cluster and may name more of the circle.
- Anything for the **Harvey Tract** (M.R. 10-77, 1886) — would give Adele a
  family; and **Tract No. 320** (1908), which would give Nina one.

## 6. ⭐⭐ UC Irvine — and why most of this may never need a trip at all

Added 2026-09-18. Kenny is on the UCI campus constantly. UCI holds almost
nothing about Los Angeles itself — but that is the wrong question. **UCI is a
UC, and the UC system's request machinery reaches into UCLA and SRLF.**

### The one that changes the economics: free chapter scans

- **UCI Scanning Service** — free scans of book chapters, requested from UC
  Library Search, **2–3 business days**: https://www.lib.uci.edu/scanning-service
- **UCI Interlibrary Loan** for items held elsewhere in UC — free, ~5–10 working
  days, **unlimited requests for SRLF-stored items**:
  https://www.lib.uci.edu/interlibrary-loan · libill@uci.edu
- Circulating UCLA/SRLF items can be **requested for pickup at UCI** — sign in to
  UC Library Search, click Request, choose the pickup campus.

⭐ **So the four compiled-ordinance volumes (§1b) may never need a drive.** Submit
chapter-scan requests for the **streets chapter** of each. All four are pre-1930
and therefore **public domain**, which removes the copyright limit that normally
caps a scan at one chapter or 10% — say so in the request note and ask for the
chapter complete, plus the index pages.

⚠ **The catch, and it is real.** Volumes whose location reads `SLF-South
Non-Circ … Request at UCLA YRL Special Collections` are UCLA Special Collections
material, and **UCLA LSC does not lend through ILL at all**
(https://ucla.libanswers.com/lsc/faq/380952). That covers the `JS…` copies.
Two routes around it, in order:

1. **Ask for the `KFX…` Law Library copies instead** — Caswell 1878, Robinson
   1884 and Teed 1889 all exist there, in a *superseded* collection rather than
   Special Collections, which is a different and much easier lending category.
   **This is the reason §1c matters even if you never visit: it identifies a
   non-Special-Collections copy of three of the four volumes.**
2. **Ask UCI ILL to fund a digitised surrogate.** The UC systemwide *Interlibrary
   Loan Needs Statement for UC Special Collections* says that where a loan is not
   feasible, reproduction "will be funded by the ILL budget to provide a
   surrogate for loan":
   https://libraries.universityofcalifornia.edu/rsc/interlibrary-loan-needs-statement-for-uc-special-collections/
   Cite that URL in the request. ⚠ Untested whether UCI applies it routinely.

### Nearby, and genuinely worth a morning

⭐ **Sherman Library, Corona del Mar** (~20 min from UCI, open daily 10:30–4):
a Southern California research library, not an Orange County one, with
**particular strength in California city directories, including street-address
directories**. LA city directories are the workhorse source for dating street
name changes, and §2 of this file is largely a directory errand. They also run a
**fee-based lookup service**, so a query can be posted rather than driven.
https://thesherman.org/library/

Also: **UCI Special Collections MS-R70**, a California newspaper collection
including **the earliest issues of the *Los Angeles Star***
(https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt367nc3n3) — the key 1851–1879 LA
paper, and on this campus. **Chapman University** is an LA as Subject member.
The **LA as Subject directory** (https://laassubject.org/directory) is the right
index for "who holds this LA map" before committing to any drive.
