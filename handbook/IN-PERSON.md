# What to look for in person

Things this project cannot get at from a keyboard: physical items in reading
rooms, and databases that are robots-blocked or JavaScript-only. Written to be
opened on a phone at a desk in a library.

**Keep this file honest about what is actually needed.** Each item says what it
would unblock, so a trip that only gets half the list still pays.

---

## 1. UCLA — the 1903/04 Chamber of Commerce city map ⭐ TOP PRIORITY

**What:** the Los Angeles Chamber of Commerce city map of 1904, **compiled and
drawn in 1903 by Félix Violé**. Held in the UCLA map room (this is the copy
George Garrigues worked from for his street-changes list, which is how we know
it is there).

**Why it is the single most valuable physical item for this project:** it is a
complete snapshot of the street names in force at one moment, right in the
middle of the period the corpus covers. Garrigues's list is derived from it but
gives only the names that *changed* by 2002. The map itself answers questions
his list structurally cannot.

**What to check on it, in priority order:**

1. **Mill Street** — is it on the 1903 map? If yes, Mill predates the 1904
   Industrial Tract and the "Molino (1896) came first, Mill is the echo"
   reading in `mill-st-arts-district` collapses. If no, that reading stands.
   This is the cleanest single question on the sheet.
2. **Mimosa Street** — confirm it is there (Garrigues implies it is) and read
   its extent. `mimosa-st` claims Tract No. 1687 of 1912 did NOT coin it.
3. **Lemon Street and Wilson Street** — are they *both* on the map, and where?
   This settles the biggest open inference in the corpus: whether Lemon Street
   was renamed Wilson in 1887 (our reading) or is a separate neighbouring
   street (Kines's). See `lemon-st-arts-district`.
4. **Ida Street** — how many are there? We have two entities and Garrigues
   implies a third, near Old Temple Road and Marathon.
5. **Alabama Street** — we know it was gone by 1903 but not what it became.
   Check the Temple–Beaudry block between W. 1st, Court, Colton and Temple.
6. **Everett Street / Everett Place**, **St. Paul Avenue / St. Paul Place** —
   in each case we have one entity and the city registry has two streets.
7. Anything lettered in the block bounded by **W. 1st, Court, Colton and
   Temple** (the 1883 state-name sheet, M.R. 5-69) and around **Boylston and
   Bixel** (the 1884 gemstone sheet, M.R. 6-115).

**Also at UCLA if there is time:** anything in the map room catalogued to
**Félix Violé** or to the Chamber of Commerce for adjacent years — a second
edition would bracket changes rather than just snapshot them.

## 2. LAPL Central, History & Genealogy — Bernice Kimball

**What:** **Bernice Kimball, *Street Names of Los Angeles*** (Bureau of
Engineering, 1988). Not online anywhere. LAPL holds it.

**Why:** it is the Bureau of Engineering's own compendium of name changes,
compiled from city records — i.e. the thing that would date the renamings this
project keeps having to infer. Kines relies on it.

**What to look up, in priority order** (all of these are entities that
currently say the date is not documented):

| Look up | What it would settle |
|---|---|
| **Colton Street** | when State Street became Colton (we guess the 1897 commission) |
| **Delong Street** | when Virginia became De Long, and who De Long was |
| **Fremont Avenue** | when Beaudry Street became Fremont — the Oct. 1890 ordinance was *vetoed*, so the real change is undated |
| **Hewitt Street** | who Hewitt was. Searched hard, never found |
| **Toluca Street** | corroborate the Oct. 1888 duplicate cull |
| **Huntley Drive** | when Sapphire Street became Huntley |
| **Margo Street** | when Palm Street became Margo |
| **Firman / Firmin Street** | which spelling is right, and when Fireman changed |
| **Imperial Street** | the Palm → Mimosa → Imperial sequence |
| **Weller Street** | who Weller was; also whether the Onizuka renaming was 1987 or 1988 |
| **Market Street / West Market Street** | West Market has no entity and we only know it from Guinn |

**Also at LAPL:** the **city directories, 1873–1907** (ResCarta, which is
JavaScript-only so we cannot touch it). The single highest-value directory
lookup is **1886–87, for "Lemon Street"** — Kines says D. C. Wilson was living
on it, and the directory entry would place it.

## 3. The Huntington — Solano-Reeve survey collection

**What:** the **Solano-Reeve collection** of surveyors' manuscript maps
(`mssSolano`). Item-level metadata is not exposed online; the reading room has
it.

**Why:** two of the best-attested naming sets in the corpus have untraced
subdividers, and the Solano-Reeve sheets for Hancock's Survey are the likeliest
place their names are written down.

**What to ask for:**

- Hancock's Survey, **Block 35** — the 1883 state-name sheet (Alabama, Ohio,
  Virginia, State). The Huntington's online listing has Blocks 17, 22, 24–25,
  36–37 and B, but **no Block 35** is exposed; ask whether one exists.
- Hancock's Survey, **Block 38** — the 1884 gemstone sheet (Diamond, Emerald,
  Jewel, Ruby, Sapphire, South Diamond). There is a catalogued item,
  *"[Los Angeles: partial street plan, showing part of Ord's Survey and Blocks
  36-39, Hancock's Survey, with surface detail]"*, which covers Block 38.
- The **Alanis Vineyard Tract** sheet — catalogued as running *"Alameda to
  Vignes St., Sainsevain St. to Labory Lane"*, surveyed *"at the request of
  I.W. Hellmann and Chas. Ducommun"*. Worth seeing whole: it georeferences the
  whole Frenchtown cluster and may name more of the circle.
- Anything for the **Harvey Tract** (M.R. 10-77, 1886) — would give Adele a
  family; and **Tract No. 320** (1908), which would give Nina one.

## 4. Anywhere with a JSTOR login — mostly done

✔ **Guinn, "The Passing of Our Historic Street Names" (1912–13)** — Kenny
obtained this 2026-09-17. It is now `documents/guinn-1912`. Nothing further
needed.

The companion piece is worth having if it is easy: **J. M. Guinn, "The Plan of
Old Los Angeles and the Story of Its Highways and Byways," Annual Publication
of the Historical Society of Southern California, Vol. III (1895)** — Guinn
refers readers to it for "the history of many of the old streets and the
changes in name", so it is the longer version of the 1912 paper.
